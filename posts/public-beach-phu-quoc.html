Which Phu Quoc Shores Charge Nothing to Walk On

Many first-time visitors to Phu Quoc assume every pretty stretch of sand sits behind a hotel gate. That is not how the island works. Most of the coastline is open to anyone who turns up, and only a handful of spots ask for money, usually for facilities rather than for the sand itself.

Open shores with no entry charge

According to the guide this post draws on, the public beaches include Long Beach, Bai Dai, Ganh Dau and Rach Vem. You can park a motorbike, walk down and swim without paying anyone. On Bai Dai in the north, the land behind the sand falls inside the national park buffer zone, so building is limited and access points tend to stay simple.

  • Long Beach on the west coast, close to Duong Dong
  • Bai Dai, roughly 15 kilometres of northern shoreline
  • Ganh Dau at the north-western tip, beside a fishing village
  • Rach Vem, the starfish shallows on the northern coast

Where you may be asked to pay

Some resort frontages, plus a few managed sections at Sao and Khem in the south, charge for loungers and showers. The sea is still the sea, but the comfortable chair and the rinse afterwards come at a cost. If you are happy on a towel, walk a little further along the arc and the managed zone usually ends.

Small habits that keep things easy

Carry your own shade and drinking water, since public stretches rarely rent umbrellas. Take rubbish home with you. Most beaches are unpatrolled, so swim close to shore and skip the water after heavy rain.

Conclusion

Paying is optional on most of the island. Know which shores are open, bring a towel and water, and you can spend a whole week by the sea without a single gate. Further reading: a shore-by-shore guide to the island&#x27;s coast.
