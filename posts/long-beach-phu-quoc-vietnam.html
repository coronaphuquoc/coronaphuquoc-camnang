Bai Truong or Bai Dai: Untangling Two Beaches With One English Name

Ask a driver on Phu Quoc to take you to "Long Beach" and there is a fair chance you will end up somewhere you did not intend. Two different shorelines carry that English label, because both Vietnamese words, truong and dai, describe length. Sorting out which one you mean before you book a room or a ride saves a lot of confusion.

Bai Truong, the commercial shore

Bai Truong is the strip most guides mean when they say Long Beach. It sits on the west coast beside Duong Dong and runs for roughly 20 km, parallel to Tran Hung Dao Street.

  • Lively, with restaurants, shops and water-sports rentals close together
  • Around 12 km from Phu Quoc International Airport, about 20 minutes by car
  • Stretches from Dinh Cau Cape in the north down to An Thoi town

Bai Dai, the quiet northern shore

Bai Dai lies in the northwest of the island, about 30 km north of Bai Truong. It is around 15 km long and far more resort-focused, with calmer sand and easy reach of North Island sights such as Rach Vem. Travellers who want a slower stay with sunset views often base themselves here instead.

How to say it to a driver

The simplest fix is to use the Vietnamese name. Saying Bai Truong points a taxi toward the Duong Dong side, while Bai Dai sends it north toward Ganh Dau. Showing the name written on your phone works even better when accents get in the way.
