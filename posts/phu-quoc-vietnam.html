Phu Quoc at a Glance: Geography and Quick Facts

Phu Quoc is a 567 square kilometre island in the Gulf of Thailand, off the south-western coast of Vietnam. It belongs to An Giang Province and is administered as the Phu Quoc Special Zone. It actually sits closer to Cambodia than to the Vietnamese mainland, which gives it a landscape and climate of its own.

The Pearl Island

Locals call it Dao Ngoc, the Pearl Island, partly for its glittering coastline and partly for a long history of pearl farming. Around 70 percent of the land is protected national park. That protection has kept the jungle interior largely intact, so a short drive inland from the coast can put you on a red-dirt road among hardwood trees and pepper farms.

Three zones to understand

  • The north, around Bai Dai and Ganh Dau, is the quieter, greener end, and it is where VinWonders, Vinpearl Safari and Grand World are clustered.
  • The west, centred on Duong Dong town and Long Beach, is the historic heart, with night markets and fish sauce factories.
  • The south leads to An Thoi town and the boats heading out to the southern archipelago.

Why the layout matters

Distances between these zones are real. Attractions that look close on a map can take a good while to reach, so it pays to check the exact location of each place before building a day around it. Many travellers search for Phu Quoc City, but the island as a whole includes Duong Dong, An Thoi, Ganh Dau, the beaches, the national park and the surrounding islets.
