Starfish Etiquette at Rach Vem: Look but Do Not Lift

The shallow bay at Rach Vem earned its nickname, the kingdom of starfish, because hundreds of red starfish rest on the sand in water so clear you can count them from the shore. That ease of access is also the problem. Every season, some visitors pull starfish into the air for a quick photo, and many of those animals do not survive it.

Why lifting them causes harm

Starfish take in oxygen through their bodies. They are very sensitive to sudden changes in temperature and sunlight, and the landing page for the village notes that a starfish removed from the water for even a few minutes can die. They are wild and not venomous, so seeing them up close is safe for you. The risk runs the other way.

Simple rules to follow in the water

  • Keep the starfish submerged at all times.
  • Do not hold them, step on them, or touch their underside.
  • Never throw one back into the water roughly.
  • Watch your footing in the shallows, since they rest right under your feet.
  • Use a waterproof phone case and shoot through the surface instead.

Getting a good photo without touching

Clear water is your friend here. In the dry season, from November to April, the bay stays calm and transparent, and early low tide, roughly between 7 and 9 in the morning, usually gives the clearest view. Hold the phone just below the surface, keep the sun behind you, and let the colour of the starfish do the work. A slow, steady shot through still water often looks better than anything held up in the air.
