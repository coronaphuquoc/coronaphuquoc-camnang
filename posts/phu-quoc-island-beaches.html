Reading the Water: Snorkel Visibility on Phu Quoc's Shores by Month

Snorkellers judge an island by one number: how far they can see underwater. On Phu Quoc that number changes a great deal with the calendar, and planning around it saves a lot of disappointment with a mask on your face.

The dry season numbers

From November to April the sea settles and visibility can reach around 15 metres in the clearest spots. Air temperatures sit between 26 and 30 °C, which means you can stay in the water for long stretches without getting cold. On Bai Dai in the north, dry-season visibility regularly reaches 8 to 10 metres, enough to watch fish over the sand without leaving the shallows.

  • March and April combine calm water with fewer visitors than the December to February peak.
  • June to September brings the south-west monsoon and choppy, cloudier water on the western side.

Where to point your fins

The An Thoi Archipelago off the southern tip holds more than 15 islands. Hon Mong Tay, Hon Gam Ghi and Hon Dam Ngang are the names you will hear for snorkelling and kayaking, reached by speedboat tours from An Thoi port. Closer to shore, Vung Bau on the north-west coast suits gentle snorkelling between two forested headlands, and Khem in the south has reliably clear water for a quick look at the seabed.

Small habits that help

Minh hoạ: phu quoc island beaches

Go out in the morning before wind ruffles the surface. After heavy rain, give the water a day to clear and avoid entering near river mouths. Most beaches are unpatrolled, so snorkel with a partner and stay within easy reach of the shore unless you are on an organised boat.
