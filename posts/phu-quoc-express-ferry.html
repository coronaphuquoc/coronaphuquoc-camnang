Phu Quoc Express Ferry Explained for First-Time Visitors

If you are heading to Phu Quoc by sea, the Phu Quoc Express ferry is one of two main operators that make the crossing from the Mekong Delta coast. Knowing what the company runs, and from where, makes the rest of the planning straightforward.

What the company operates

Phu Quoc Express uses twin-hull catamarans with aluminium alloy hulls and Rolls-Royce MTU engines, each carrying around 300 passengers. The two hulls slice through swell instead of riding over it, which cuts down the rolling motion that unsettles stomachs. Inside there are two zones: economy, and a VIP area with leather reclining seats, screens and extra room.

Two mainland departure points

  • Ha Tien: a shorter hop of about 1 hour 10 to 1 hour 30 minutes, around 45 nautical miles.
  • Rach Gia: roughly 120 km, taking about 2 hours 20 to 2 hours 30 minutes.
  • Both routes finish at Bai Vong Port on the east coast of the island.

Who should pick which route

Travellers arriving from An Giang, Chau Doc or overland from Cambodia are closer to Ha Tien, so that route saves road time. Anyone coming from Ho Chi Minh City or the central Mekong Delta generally finds Rach Gia easier, since it is a larger port city with stronger road links. The longer Rach Gia run on a catamaran also tends to feel steadier than a short, choppy crossing.

A note on cars

The fast boats take passengers and motorbikes only. Drivers bringing a car need the slower car ferry from Ha Tien, a detail that catches people out every week.

Conclusion
